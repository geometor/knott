KNOTT-1.1 — Internal & External Golden Section Constructions on a Segment¶

Given a finite segment AB, midpoint-circle compass-and-straightedge constructions divide AB internally at G so AB : AG = AG : GB = Φ and extend AB externally to E so AE : AB = AB : BE = Φ, erecting Golden Rectangle AEFD.

Constructive Synthesis & Exact Proof¶

Dr. Ron Knott opens his 2D Golden Geometry survey (KnottPhi2D) by pairing the two complementary compass-and-straightedge problems of Euclid (EuclidElements, Books II.11 and VI.30): extending a given straight segment externally in extreme and mean ratio, and dividing the same segment internally.

  1. External Golden Extension: Starting from given segment AB of length \(2\) and erecting square ABCD, bisect the base at M so segment MC has exact quadrance \(Q(M, C) = 1^2 + 2^2 = 5\). Sweeping guide circle (M, C) onto line [AB] beyond B determines E with AE \(= 1 + \sqrt{5} = 2\Phi\) and BE \(= \sqrt{5} - 1 = 2\phi\), completing Golden Rectangle AEFD above F.

  2. Internal Golden Division: Joining A to midpoint H of \(BC\) produces AH of length \(\sqrt{5}\). Unit circle (H, B) cuts AH at K (\(AK = \sqrt{5} - 1 = 2\phi\)), and circle (A, K) transfers that length onto the base at internal golden cut G. Thus major internal segment AG and external overhang BE are equal (\(2\phi\)), leaving minor segment GB \(= 2\phi^2\) and fixing the projective cross-ratio \((A, E; G, B) = \phi^2 = 2 - \Phi\).
