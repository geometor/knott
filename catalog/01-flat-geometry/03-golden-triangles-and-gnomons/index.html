KNOTT-1.3 — Golden Triangles (72°–72°–36°) & Golden Gnomons (36°–36°–108°)¶

An isosceles acute golden triangle ABC with base AB = 1 and legs AC = BC = Φ is partitioned by base-angle bisector AD (with AD = CD = 1 and BD = φ) into an obtuse golden gnomon CAD and a self-similar acute golden triangle DAB with exact spreads s(36°) = (5 − √5)/8 and s(72°) = (5 + √5)/8.

Constructive Synthesis & Exact Proof¶

In Elements IV.10 (EuclidElements), Euclid constructs an isosceles triangle having each base angle double the apex angle; Dr. Ron Knott (KnottPhi2D) develops its paired acute/obtuse gnomon recursion as the backbone of pentagonal geometry.

  • Acute Golden Triangle & Bisector Deflation: Over unit base AB, sweeping radius \(\Phi\) from A and B fixes apex C of acute golden triangle ABC, whose legs AC and BC are equal with quadrance \(Q = \Phi^2 = \Phi + 1\). Intersecting leg BC with unit circle (A, B) at D makes bisector AD and base AB equal (\(AD = AB = CD = 1\)), leaving \(BD = \Phi - 1 = \phi\).

  • Self-Similar Cascade & Exact Spreads: Triangle ABC splits into obtuse golden gnomon \(\triangle CAD\) (\(36^\circ\text{–}36^\circ\text{–}108^\circ\)) and daughter acute golden triangle ABD of area ratio \(\Phi : 1\). Sweeping circle (D, B) onto AC at E (DE \(= \phi\)) repeats the deflation ad infinitum, with Norman J. Wildberger’s exact pentagonal spreads \(s(36^\circ) = (5 - \sqrt{5})/8\) and \(s(72^\circ) = (5 + \sqrt{5})/8\) (Wildberger2005).
