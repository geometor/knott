KNOTT-1.6 — Penrose Kites & Darts (P2) and Thick & Thin Golden Rhombs (P3)¶

A thick 72°–108° golden rhomb of side Φ divides at unit distance K along its long diagonal of length Φ² into a convex Penrose kite KBCD and a non-convex Penrose dart ABKD with exact area ratio Φ : 1, matching the Φ : 1 area ratio between thick (72°) and thin (36°) Penrose golden rhombs.

Constructive Synthesis & Exact Proof¶

Roger Penrose (Penrose1974, Gardner1977) reduced aperiodic five-fold planar tiling to two golden prototile families cataloged by Dr. Ron Knott (KnottPhi2D): P2 (John Conway’s kite and dart) and P3 (thick and thin golden rhombs).

  • P2 Kite & Dart Dissection: Inside thick \(72^\circ\text{–}108^\circ\) rhomb \(ABCD\) whose four sides AB, BC, CD, and DA are equal of length \(\Phi\), long diagonal AC has length \(\Phi^2 = \Phi + 1\). Marking K along AC so AK, BK, and DK are equal to \(1\) (leaving kite spine KC \(= \Phi\)) partitions the rhomb into convex kite KBCD (two acute golden triangles) and non-convex dart ABKD (two obtuse golden gnomons) in exact area ratio \(\Phi : 1\).

  • P3 Thick & Thin Golden Rhombs: Adjoining thin \(36^\circ\text{–}144^\circ\) rhomb AFEB along shared edge AB (with edges AF, FE, and EB of length \(\Phi\)) yields interior spreads \(s(72^\circ) = (5+\sqrt{5})/8\) vs. \(s(36^\circ) = (5-\sqrt{5})/8\) and the identical area ratio \(\sin 72^\circ / \sin 36^\circ = 2\cos 36^\circ = \Phi\).
