KNOTT-1.5 — Kepler’s Golden Right Triangle (1, √Φ, Φ) & Circle-Square Near-Quadrature¶

A right triangle with side lengths (1, √Φ, Φ) has side quadrances (1, Φ, Φ²) simultaneously in geometric progression and Pythagorean sum 1 + Φ = Φ², satisfies cos θ = tan θ = 1/√Φ, and equates the perimeter 4√Φ of the square on leg √Φ with the circumference πΦ of the circle on diameter Φ to within 0.096%.

Constructive Synthesis & Exact Proof¶

In an October 1597 letter to Michael Mästlin, Johannes Kepler combined the Pythagorean theorem with the Golden Ratio to isolate the unique right triangle whose sides form a geometric progression (KnottPhi2D).

  • Euclidean Geometric-Mean Construction: Along diameter segment from D \((-1, 0)\) through B \((0, 0)\) to E \((\Phi, 0)\), Thales semicircle (O, E) cuts the vertical axis at C \((0, \sqrt{\Phi})\) (Elements VI.13). In Kepler triangle DBC, short leg BD, long leg BC, and hypotenuse DC (which is equal to golden segment BE, joined by chord CE) have exact quadrances \((1, \Phi, \Phi^2)\) satisfying both \(Q_{BC}^2 = Q_{BD} Q_{DC}\) and \(1 + \Phi = \Phi^2\).

  • Coincidence of Cosine & Tangent and Near-Quadrature: At acute apex C, \(\cos\theta_C = \tan\theta_C = \sin\theta_D = 1/\sqrt{\Phi}\) (\(\cos^2\theta_C = \tan^2\theta_C = \phi\)). Erecting square BGFC (via F and G) of perimeter \(4\sqrt{\Phi} \approx 5.08808\) alongside circle (M, C) centered at hypotenuse midpoint M of circumference \(\pi\Phi \approx 5.08320\) exhibits the celebrated circle–square perimeter near-quadrature highlighted by Dr. Knott (\(4 / (\pi\sqrt{\Phi}) \approx 1.000959\), within \(0.096\%\)).
