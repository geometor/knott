KNOTT-5.3 — Wythoff’s Array, Beatty’s Complementary Sequences (⌊nΦ⌋, ⌊nΦ²⌋), & Stolarsky’s Array¶

Because 1/Φ + 1/Φ² = 1, Beatty’s sequences Aₙ = ⌊nΦ⌋ and Bₙ = ⌊nΦ²⌋ = Aₙ + n partition Z_{>0} without overlap; seeding generalized Fibonacci rows via W(r, c) = ⌊(r + 1)Φ⌋ F_{c+2} + r F_{c+1} (Wythoff) or nearest-integer multiples [kΦ] (Stolarsky) places every positive integer in a unique (row, col) cell.

Constructive Synthesis & Exact Proof¶

Because \(\frac{1}{\Phi} + \frac{1}{\Phi^2} = \phi + \phi^2 = 1\), Samuel Beatty’s theorem guarantees that lower and upper Beatty sequences \(A_n = \lfloor n\Phi \rfloor\) and \(B_n = \lfloor n\Phi^2 \rfloor = A_n + n\) partition \(\mathbb{Z}_{>0}\) into disjoint cold positions of Willem Wythoff’s game (Wythoff1907, KnottFibGen).

  • Exact Wythoff & Beatty Cone: From origin O, exact rays OR1 (\(y = \Phi x\)) and OR2 (\(y = \Phi^2 x\)) bound the cold Wythoff pairs W1 \((1, 2)\), W2 \((3, 5)\), W3 \((4, 7)\), and W4 \((6, 10)\) (connected along W1W2, W2W3, and W3W4 beneath ideal targets A1 – A4).

  • Bijective Wythoff & Stolarsky Interspersions: Seeding row \(r \ge 0\) of the Wythoff array with \(W(r, c) = \lfloor (r+1)\Phi \rfloor F_{c+2} + r F_{c+1}\) groups integers whose Zeckendorf representations end in \(c\) trailing zeros (Morrison1980), while Kenneth Stolarsky’s nearest-integer Golden Ratio rounding \(b = \lfloor a\Phi + 1/2 \rfloor\) (Stolarsky1977) yields the dual Stolarsky array studied by John Conway and Clark Kimberling.
